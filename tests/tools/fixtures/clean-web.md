# Salt or fish (or salted fish)? New insights from Caprolace settlement (Alessandri et al. 2019)

This article reports a 2017 excavation on two small islands in the Caprolace lagoon, central Italy. The authors argue that the southern island held a Middle Bronze Age workshop, most likely making salt by briquetage. They also suggest that fish products may have been processed at similar coastal sites.

## Background

- Briquetage boils brine in special vessels, which are then broken to take out the salt cakes. (sec. "2.1 Salt production in Europe")
- Before this study, no Bronze Age site in Italy had produced evidence of fish processing. (sec. "2.6 Evidence of fish consumption and processing in Italy")

## Results

- Wood from 94–96 cm deep in the peat core dates to 1606–1433 cal BCE. (sec. "4.1 Environmental reconstruction")
- Layer SU 202 was only partly excavated, so the authors cannot say whether its pottery sample is representative. (sec. "4.2 Pottery typo-chronology and contexts")
- Sherd CP 131 held the largest salt microcrystals, up to 0.3–0.4 mm, inside its microcracks. (Fig. 20)

## Discussion

- The authors suggest that SU 104 and part of SU 103 are debris from briquetage, though the chemistry was not conclusive. (sec. "5.1 Content and use")
- They suggest the “fish hypothesis” might be a viable line of research for the other vessel shapes. (sec. "6. Conclusion")
