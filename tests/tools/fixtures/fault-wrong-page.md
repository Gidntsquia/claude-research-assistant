# Salt or fish (or salted fish)? New insights from Caprolace settlement (Alessandri et al. 2019)

This article reports a 2017 excavation on two small islands in the Caprolace lagoon, central Italy. The authors argue that the southern island held a Middle Bronze Age workshop, most likely making salt by briquetage. They also suggest that fish products may have been processed at similar coastal sites.

## Background

- Briquetage boils brine in special vessels, which are then broken to take out the salt cakes. (p. 2)
  > “which were then broken to extract the salt cakes”
- Before this study, no Bronze Age site in Italy had produced evidence of fish processing. (p. 7)
  > “evidence of fish processing has never been found in Bronze Age Italian sites”

## Results

- Wood from 94–96 cm deep in the peat core dates to 1606–1433 cal BCE. (p. 10)
  > “Results from the 14C analyses are 1606–1433 cal BCE”
- Layer SU 202 was only partly excavated, so the authors cannot say whether its pottery sample is representative. (p. 13)
  > “it is not possible to know whether our sample is statistically representative”
- Sherd CP 131 held the largest salt microcrystals, up to 0.3–0.4 mm, inside its microcracks. (p. 12)
  > “in CP 131 we found the largest NaCl microcrystals”

## Discussion

- The authors suggest that SU 104 and part of SU 103 are debris from briquetage, though the chemistry was not conclusive. (p. 28)
  > “we suggest that SU 104 and partially SU 103 are the debris of a briquetage activity”
- They suggest the “fish hypothesis” might be a viable line of research for the other vessel shapes. (p. 31)
  > “might be a viable line of research”
